    “20% lower risk”
    still needs a denominator.

    Baseline10 in 100
    After intervention8 in 100
    02550 in 100
    RELATIVE REDUCTION20%
    ABSOLUTE REDUCTION2 percentage points

    Step 1 Create one living health brief
    

    Peter’s repository organizes the brief into patient information, up to three next actions, known facts, explanations of medical terms, and a compact care log. Keep the current snapshot short enough to scan during an appointment; place detailed history and old decisions in the log. For routine health, substitute ongoing conditions, prevention decisions, and personal goals for cancer-specific details. Repository
    

    Set up the workspace
    

    The simplest route is a private document plus an AI chat. Paste the current brief into each new relevant conversation, or attach it with the reports. ChatGPT Projects can keep related chats, sources, and instructions together; upload or connect the sources explicitly. A project does not automatically gain access to a folder on your computer. Official OpenAI documentation
    

    For Peter’s packaged workflow, open his repository and review its instructions. In a compatible agent with skill-installation support, his documented installation request is:
    

    Install the /fuck-cancer skill globally from https://github.com/petergyang/fuck-cancer

    

    His repository also documents this terminal alternative for compatible environments with Node.js and npx:
    

    npx skills add petergyang/fuck-cancer --skill fuck-cancer --global --yes

    

    A skill is a reusable set of instructions and resources. Installing it does not grant medical expertise, automatically connect records, or grant permission to send them. Peter’s Google Doc workflow needs a supported connected Google Drive integration; the repository also supports a local Markdown file. If your interface cannot install skills, use the prompts here directly. Peter’s installation instructions · Skills explained
    

    Copy this brief prompt
    

    Help me prepare a health-navigation brief for [me / my consenting relative].
Our immediate decision is [decision and date]. Use only the attached records and my notes for personal facts.

Create these sections:
1. Essential patient information, current medicines, allergies, and care-team contacts.
2. Up to three next actions, each with an owner, due date, and completion condition.
3. What we know, with document name, date, and page or section for every important fact.
4. Plain-English definitions of unfamiliar terms.
5. A dated care log, keeping the current snapshot separate from older history.

Distinguish confirmed facts, patient-reported information, pending results, unknowns, and research hypotheses. Preserve units, dates, negations, and uncertainty. Flag conflicting records instead of resolving them by guessing. Ask only questions that could change the immediate next step. Do not diagnose, choose treatment, or invent missing information.

    Step 2 Organize records and close the handoffs
    

    Peter separates three requests because the materials may be held by different departments. A written imaging report describes a scan; it does not replace the scan itself. DICOM means Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine, a standard format for medical images. Pathology slides and blocks contain physical tissue material and require the laboratory’s handling process. Peter’s records workflow

    Ask each institution about its process, authorization form, cost, expected timing, and delivery method. Peter’s experience with delays motivates early requests; your hospital’s estimate is the one to track. Ask the receiving specialist what is required before requesting broad archives or transferring limited tissue.
    

    Keep a small records index
    

    Use folders such as Brief, Reports, Imaging, Pathology, Appointments, and Research. Name written files consistently, for example 2026-11-04_Hospital_PathologyReport.pdf. Preserve the original file and create a separately labeled summary. Keep an index with document date, institution, record type, location, and verification status. Do not rename internal DICOM files or rearrange their structure casually.
    

    From this records index, identify the missing material needed for [appointment or decision]. Draft separate requests for written records, original imaging, and pathology material only if relevant. For each request, list the official department, authorization needed, likely delivery method, and questions about turnaround and fees. Verify contact details on the institution’s own website. Draft only; I will review and send.

    

    A request is complete when the handoff is verified
    

    For each referral or test, track these states: requested, acknowledged, scheduled or processing, received, reviewed, and action agreed. Record the responsible person and the next check date. This is an added coordination technique, not a clinical scoring system.
    

    An example: “Referral faxed” does not confirm that the clinic accepted it. “Report uploaded” does not confirm that the specialist reviewed it. Ask: “Has the referral arrived, is anything missing, and when should we expect a decision?” For a result, ask who reviews it and how you will receive the plan. Silence alone is not proof of a normal result.
    

    

    Step 3 Research the decision that is actually open
    

    In the video, Peter explores standard treatment, a second opinion, tumor sequencing, and trials. These are possible workstreams, not requirements for everyone. Before researching, state what is known about the diagnosis, its extent, previous treatment, relevant test results, overall health, location, and the patient’s goals. Missing decision-critical details should stay visible.
    

    Understand usual care and seek an appropriate second opinion
    

    Begin with the official health agency for your country and condition. For cancer, the National Cancer Institute’s PDQ summaries are public evidence summaries. Ask your treating clinician which professional guidelines apply to your case. Match a second-opinion specialist to the exact disease or unresolved decision, rather than to fame alone. Distinguish a review of tissue diagnosis from a review of the treatment plan. Check insurance, referral rules, remote-review options, and whether any delay is medically acceptable. NCI finding cancer care · PDQ summaries
    

    Peter mentions major US centers, including UCSF, Stanford, MD Anderson, Memorial Sloan Kettering, and Dana-Farber. Use the NCI center directory as a starting point, then verify the relevant program’s current intake requirements. A second opinion can confirm the existing plan as well as suggest changes. NCI
    

    For the decision [specific question], summarize the usual care options supported by current public official sources in [country]. Use this verified brief for context. For each option, explain the intended benefit, meaningful harms and burdens, evidence population, and what must be confirmed before it applies. Link sources and dates. Separate approved care, off-label use, investigational care, and unsupported claims. Finish with three questions for the clinician, not a treatment recommendation.

    

    Ask what tumor testing would change
    

    A biomarker is a measurable biological feature, such as a protein or genetic change. Tumor profiling looks for features that may help select treatment; inherited or germline testing asks whether a person was born with a genetic change that may also matter to relatives. They answer different questions. Testing may find no actionable result, and an uncertain variant should not determine treatment. NCI biomarker testing
    

    Ask: “Which testing has already been done? Would additional testing change a current choice? Is the available tissue sufficient? Would a new biopsy be needed? What are the turnaround, coverage, and alternatives? Would any finding warrant genetic counseling?” Broad sequencing and tissue storage are not automatic next steps for every reader. Peter’s vendor and expense are anecdotes, not endorsements.
    

    Search clinical trials without assuming eligibility
    

    Clinical trials test interventions in people. Phase 1 generally examines safety and dose; phase 2 looks for activity while continuing safety evaluation; phase 3 compares approaches; phase 4 studies treatment after approval. “Phase” describes research stage, not cancer stage. All phases involve uncertainty and possible harms. NCI trial phases
    

    ClinicalTrials.gov includes studies in the US and other countries; it is not an exhaustive list of every trial worldwide. Search by condition, relevant markers or treatment setting, and a feasible travel radius. Inspect eligibility, the study’s overall status, and the individual location. A recruiting label is not confirmation that a particular person can enroll. Contact the study team for screening with the treating clinician’s input. Patient search help
    

    Find up to three trial candidates for this verified diagnosis in [country / region / travel radius]. Use official registry records and current center pages. For each, give the registry identifier and link, intervention, phase, overall status, nearest site's status, key eligibility requirements, date checked, travel burden, and contact. Mark missing facts and potential exclusions. Compare the question being tested with available usual care. Never say the patient is eligible; list what the site must confirm.

    

    Bring a table to the appointment with columns for candidate, why it might fit, missing facts, standard-care alternative, burden, and next verification. Ask about random assignment, additional tests, visit frequency, research-related costs, routine-care costs, and whether starting another treatment changes eligibility. Research can happen in parallel with care; it should not delay time-sensitive evaluation or treatment.
    

    Make AI show its evidence
    

    Check who published the information, when it was reviewed, what population was studied, what outcome was measured, and whether the source sells the intervention. Open the cited page and verify the claim yourself. A real citation can still refer to a different disease, dose, population, or outcome. MedlinePlus evidence checklist
    

    Ask for absolute benefit, time horizon, harms, and uncertainty. For illustration only, reducing a risk from 10 in 100 to 8 in 100 is a 20% relative reduction but a 2-percentage-point absolute reduction. The implied number needed to treat is 50 over that same period, assuming the comparison is valid. These invented numbers describe no actual treatment. The website’s exercise lets readers explore this distinction.

    Step 5 Protect quality of life and family time
    

    Peter describes spending a week with his mother, taking walks, sharing meals, and calling his parents daily. The transferable step is to protect activities the patient values and use AI to reduce administrative load. Ask the person what feels supportive; a full schedule of well-intentioned help can itself become tiring. Video at 15:41
    

    An added planning practice: write down three priorities, such as comfort, staying independent, or attending a family event. Ask how each care option affects them. Agree on who handles records, transport, questions, and backup care. Choose a reasonable research window and a separate time for ordinary life, adjusted to urgency and energy.
    

    Palliative care addresses symptoms, emotional needs, and quality of life during serious illness. It can accompany active cancer treatment and does not require waiting until treatment ends. Ask whether it would help with pain, fatigue, sleep, coping, or caregiver strain. NCI palliative care
    

    Help us make a manageable week around these confirmed appointments and the patient's priorities. Ask what energy and practical support are available. Separate essential care tasks, delegable administration, rest, and one valued family activity. Suggest a backup if symptoms or fatigue change. Do not change medical instructions or schedule anything without review.

    Build a clinician-reviewed prevention register
    

    MyHealthfinder offers US screening and vaccination guidance. Use it to prepare a conversation, then tailor decisions to age, relevant anatomy, pregnancy where applicable, family and personal history, previous results, and your country’s guidance. Symptoms require clinical assessment rather than simply waiting for a screening date.
    

    Create a register with service, reason, last completed date, next due date, deciding clinician, and source. Include only agreed or explicitly undecided items. Record “discuss at next visit” when the appropriate interval is unknown. For Medicare users, a wellness visit can help create a prevention plan; additional services may have separate costs. MyHealthfinder wellness visit
    

    Prepare a prevention-review agenda from my verified age, health history, family history, medicines, vaccines, and prior screening results. Use current official guidance for [country]. Separate likely applicable services, decisions needing individual discussion, and items already current. Do not invent due dates or recommend a blanket test panel. For each proposed topic, state what missing information could change it. Ask my clinician to confirm the final plan.

    

    More testing is not automatically more prevention. False alarms, incidental findings, and follow-up procedures can create harm. Ask what a proposed test would change and what evidence supports using it in someone like you. Whole-body CT screening illustrates these risks; it should not be confused with targeted, evidence-based screening or imaging for symptoms. FDA discussion
    

    Turn health goals into sustainable actions
    

    Choose one feasible behavior with the clinician’s input when needed: activity, food habits, sleep routine, tobacco cessation, or medication organization. Define what you will do, when, and how to tell whether it happened. For adults 65 and older, CDC’s general activity guidance includes at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly, strength work on at least two days, and balance activity; adapt to abilities and conditions. This is not an exercise prescription during active treatment or unstable illness. CDC older-adult activity
    

    For chronic care, agree on which measurements matter, how to collect them, target ranges, and when to contact the team. Keep date, time, units, device, and relevant context. AI can format a trend for review; it should not diagnose from a noisy wearable reading or adjust medication. Bring new symptoms and persistent changes to the appropriate professional.
    

    

    Make every appointment produce a usable plan
    

    Before the visit, update the brief and choose three questions. Bring the medication list and relevant reports. Ask permission before recording; follow the clinic’s policy and applicable rules. A companion can take written notes instead.
    

    During the visit, ask: “What is the main decision today? What are the options and tradeoffs? What happens next, who is responsible, and by when? Which symptoms should trigger an urgent call?” Repeat the plan in your own words and invite correction.
    

    After the visit, compare your notes with the official instructions. Capture medication changes, tests, referrals, dates, responsible people, symptom instructions, and unresolved questions. If two instructions conflict, ask the clinic to reconcile them. Update the current brief and preserve the prior plan in the log.
    

    Turn these appointment notes and official instructions into a draft action list. Separate explicit clinician instructions from my interpretation and unresolved questions. Preserve medication details exactly; flag conflicts. Include owner, due date, and completion condition. Produce a short portal-message draft for any ambiguity. Do not infer that a missing instruction means a medicine should stop. I will verify the list before acting.

    

    

    Protect privacy and prevent avoidable AI errors
    

    Ask the patient which records may be used, who may see them, and whether they want AI involved. Use approved portal proxy access rather than sharing passwords. Review each product’s current privacy, retention, training, deletion, and sharing settings before uploading records. Keep full identifiers in the secure clinical context where needed; use only the minimum necessary medical facts for research. Consumer apps do not automatically receive the same HIPAA protections as a healthcare provider. HIPAA is the US Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act.     What to explore next
    

    Start with one appointment cycle and see whether the system reduces confusion. Then add a prevention register or clinician-agreed monitoring table. A useful solo project is a source-linked brief that highlights changes between visits and flags missing owners or follow-up dates, with every clinical statement reviewed by a person. Judge it by factual accuracy and handoffs completed, not by how much medical advice it generates.
